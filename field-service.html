Case study · No. 1

Field service app

Redesigning the work-order software I used every day in the field into a dashboard built for the truck, not the office.

Role
UX/UI Designer, UX Researcher
Tools
Figma, Figma Make, Claude
Timeline
2026 · 6 weeks

Context

Like many trades, the natural gas distribution industry is dangerous, high stakes, and fast paced. With so much invested in tools designed to keep technicians safe in the field, the tools they use to document their work are often overlooked.

I spent 4.5 years in the field using an SAP Asset Manager app every day. I worked as a service technician in a busy territory. Each day I logged into my app, synced to populate my work, then I would manually go over each order adding them to my GPS to try and decide the best route while considering appointment windows, proximity, and due dates. Service turn on orders were the priority, unless an emergency was dispatched to me in which case I would drop everything else to respond within one hour. Although the program did what it was supposed to, it left a lot to be desired for techs in the field.

A generic system fails to deliver

The app stores all of the data that a tech needs to do their job, but it breaks down on its delivery. Techs can receive up to 20 types of work orders daily varying in importance, displayed in a single unfiltered list.

With hundreds of techs using the software at the same time downtime is common, but no offline mode meant every outage turned into a double entry: paper in the field, re-entered later to clear orders.

For departments like billing, mapping, and compliance it did exactly what it needed to do, but for the techs in the field it fell short. This redesign focuses on structural improvements within the constraints of the current software rather than a full software overhaul.

Doing the field work

From a simple meter read to a leak investigation, it’s the same tedious form. Fields highlighted in blue should be moved to the job details page. Fields highlighted in red do not need to be included on this form, and the orange fields are conditional.

I can tell you from personal experience how hard it can be to search a busy screen for the information you need after being in the sun for a few hours. Reducing the amount of information on each screen to what is most necessary for each step of the process helps ensure accuracy and efficiency.

  • Remove from form
  • Conditional

Understanding the work flow

The benefit of having been the end user of this software is knowing what the issues are for myself and my co-workers and understanding the various work flows throughout the company.

Turning user research and experience into solutions

Narrowing my scope and focusing on the issues that mattered the most was more like scratching an itch than making a decision. I created design solutions for two major challenges techs face daily, starting with:

Problem:

Lack of decision support tools

Filters that don’t work for techs, lack of visual hierarchy across work-orders, and no GPS routing integrations leave the decision making up to the techs.

Solution:

Built-in decision support

Filters for key information, visual hierarchy of work, GPS integration and optimized route suggestion.

Problem:

Fragmented workflow

Techs are expected to complete pre-job safety briefings and track their time on every job, both requiring a separate app. Job procedures can be found online and can be a crucial resource especially for new techs, but they live deep within the company website creating a navigational headache.

Solution:

Consolidated workflow

My redesign addresses this by utilizing features already supported within the existing SAP app to bring these key resources under one umbrella. Automating things like time sheets and inventory reduce human error and improve accuracy allowing techs to show up less distracted and more prepared.

Project implications

At the conclusion of my design process, I presented my research findings to my manager, highlighting the challenges technicians face in the field and these high-fidelity mock-ups to present potential solutions.

Upper management expressed strong interest in incorporating many of our ideas into a new version of the asset management app that is currently being scoped.

Proposed changes promise to:

  • Prioritize tasks based on due dates, appointment windows, and emergency status
  • Improve accuracy of time sheet tracking
  • Improve inventory management and accuracy
  • Reduce cognitive load, helping techs manage work orders and emergency situations more safely and effectively
  • Save time across daily operations
