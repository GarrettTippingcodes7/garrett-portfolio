Case study · No. 2

Studio Six Closets

Redesigning a homepage so a team's attention to detail reads before a client ever walks in.

Role
UX/UI Designer & Researcher
Tools
Figma, FigJam, Claude
Timeline
2026 · Ongoing

Context

Studio Six is a small, owner-led custom closet design-build-install business in Orlando. I was the solo UX strategist and designer, working with a four-week timeline, no research budget, and one stakeholder. The owner Coy is my father, who was gracious enough to allow me to work with him in a professional setting with high stakes for his business.

The current home page

Most of Studio Six's new leads come from the website. The site showed customers what Studio Six does, but left them unclear on price and process. Typically the owner, Coy, spends about an hour answering questions and measuring the space during a consultation. Roughly 1 in 4 customers don't move forward once they learn the price.

The goals

Primary goal

Increase qualified consultation bookings

Metrics: consultation form submissions and call clicks, the form completion rate, and the share of consultations that fit the budget or close.

Secondary goal

Improve local search visibility

Metrics: organic impressions and clicks in Search Console, and rankings for “custom closets + [city]” terms.

What I designed, and what I shelved

Designed

Built now

  • Descriptive split hero
  • Range-based pricing
  • Step-by-step process
  • Consultation request form
Shelved

Self-service configurator

High build effort, unclear return, and a risk of IP theft, so I put that effort into the contact page instead.

Shelved

“For Builders” page

Homeowners are most of the owner’s business, and I don't yet know what builders would look for, so I parked this page for now.

The redesigned homepage

The new home page removes the fast moving carousel and replaces it with a split hero that is more descriptive and adds more pathways to a consultation, or more information about the business and process.

Due to the nature of a bespoke purchase, a price range or even a “starting at” price would likely be misleading. Instead, I included an explanation about the variable pricing model while ensuring the customer knows they will not be expected to pay for anything unless they approve the design.

I laid out the whole process from start to finish in steps so visitors know what to expect after requesting a consultation and addressed any fears of hidden fees.

A consultation form people can find

The existing consultation form was hidden in the about page. I redesigned the basic form to give the user the option to give additional information, upload photos, and disclose their budget. This page became the primary CTA on every page across the site.

What the research showed

A stakeholder interview with the owner, a competitive analysis, and secondary research uncovered 4 key challenges.

01

Trust vs. clarity

VALIDATED+
  • Coy's own account of what wins client trust
  • Person-bound trust pattern across three competitor review sets
  • Repeat client's loyalty tied to Coy, not the brand
02

Price opacity and cost anxiety

PARTLY VALIDATED+
  • Coy confirms opacity is a deliberate strategy
  • No local competitor publishes pricing (8 of 8 checked)
  • Conversion impact rests on one vendor source with a stake in the answer

Magnitude needs independent corroboration and homeowner testing.

03

Self-qualification before free consultations

NEEDS RESEARCH+
  • Pain point inferred from the stakeholder's lens and competitor patterns
  • No primary homeowner data yet on self-qualification behavior

Weakest grounding of the four. Next: homeowner interviews.

04

Design theft vs. portfolio detail depth

PARTLY VALIDATED+
  • Coy's first-hand account of shoppers taking designs to cheaper fabricators
  • Frequency and scale of copying not independently verified

Core fact grounded. Scale is directional only.

How I used AI

AI handled the grunt work so I could spend my time on decisions. Here’s where it helped, and where I took over.

Outcome

Having built a prototype, I’m presenting the designs to the stakeholder, and testing with real customers. Once completed, I will be building the website myself. Site analytics were not installed on the current website, so I have installed Google Analytics plug-ins and will compare the data to the new site when it is live.
